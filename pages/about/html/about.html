About

Engineer first, and a stubborn advocate for the person using the screen.

I’m Jamie Tucker, a full-stack software engineer in Sunnyvale, California. I spent three years at Google and YouTube on teams where the front end was the product: if the interface was confusing or slow, nothing else we built mattered.

The short version

How I got here

I was born in Florida, grew up mostly in Virginia and Michigan, and went to The Ohio State University for a B.S. in Computer Science and Engineering. On campus I was active in the National Society of Black Engineers, the Lambda Psi Engineering Honorary, and the Morrill Scholars Program — the places where I learned that engineering is a team sport before it is a technical one.

I joined Google straight out of school on Modern Creators and Formats, helping launch Google Discover ‘Notes’ to more than a million users in the US and India. From there I moved to Search Intelligence, where I owned creator verification flows and wrote the component library that made them consistent. Most recently I was on YouTube Playables, where I built the client foundation for a flagship generative AI game creation product from an empty repository.

Across all three teams the pattern was the same: arrive early in a product’s life, decide how the client should be structured, and make it possible for a lot of engineers to ship into it at once without the experience falling apart.

How I work

Four things I believe about front-end work

Architecture is a people problem

Module boundaries decide how fast a team can move. I design for the second and third engineer on the file, not just the first.

If it isn’t logged, it didn’t ship

I instrument critical journeys as part of the feature, not after it. Telemetry at 90%+ reliability is how you tell a launch from a rumour.

Research beats opinion

I sit in UX sessions and read the feedback myself. Thirty research sessions settle arguments that thirty meetings cannot.

Accessibility is table stakes

Keyboard paths, contrast, and semantics come first. A flow that only works for some users is an unfinished flow.
